| By 2030, dementia will impact 78 million individuals globally. Alzheimer's disease (AD), which precedes mild cognitive impairment (MCI), affects 60–70% of people. Currently, individuals in rural regions of nations with limited resources lack the financial resources to access expensive brain imaging or specialized experts required for screening. Automated screening could be implemented with voice analysis, which is feasible solely in developed nations. Therefore, this project exploits the complex structure of the Vietnamese language system to optimize diagnosis by focusing on cognitive domains such as memory, attention, mathematics, and language, facilitating the identification of dementia in millions of Vietnamese speakers who are presently ignored by existing technology. The main outcome will be non-invasive, cost-effective screening technique that incorporates advanced Japanese speech analysis methods through international collaboration and also produces adapted speech tasks for Vietnamese individuals with limited education. This study introduces a novel early detection strategy that is culturally accessible for those with limited opportunities and serves as an initial framework for similar procedures in low- and middle-income countries globally. |

| Current cognitive screening is diagnosed on conventional neuropsychological assessments, which require trained clinicians and exhibit inter-rater variability. However, speech-based evaluation and analysis allow patients to independently perform standardized tasks, providing objective and consistent assessments. Therefore, the objective of this research is to establish a speech-based screening protocol that is culturally appropriate for the elderly Vietnamese population, such as sustained phonation, sentence reading, picture description, story recall, and verbal fluency tasks. Specifically, this study aims to evaluate the feasibility of deploying a speech-based assessment protocol for AD and MCI within Vietnamese healthcare environments and to identify specific acoustic and linguistic speech characteristics that effectively differentiate between AD, MCI, and healthy control groups during targeted speech tasks. Our approach addresses a critical accessibility challenge, since numerous individuals in rural and poor regions often lack awareness of early cognitive decline symptoms and postpone seeking medical assistance until significant damage arises. This method facilitates remote, economical screening using mobile and telemedicine platforms to identify cognitive decline early, thus alleviating the substantial healthcare burden and annual costs associated with late-stage dementia treatment. Additionally, our protocol establishes a basis for scalable telemedicine applications in Vietnam and supplies a replicable framework for creating speech methods across the Southeast Asia, thus facilitating equitable access to early cognitive screening in nations with insufficient clinical infrastructure. |

| In Vietnam, the validated Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) are the primary standardized neuropsychological tests that are currently used for cognitive screening [1, 2]. These assessments are low-cost and widely used in clinical practice, but they have several drawbacks, including inter-rater variability, trained healthcare professionals, and rural Vietnam's elderly low-educated population. Advanced neuroimaging techniques like MRI and PET scans are accurate but cost approximately 13,955-139,547 JPY per assessment and require radiological expertise that is scarce in rural areas, where elderly people have the highest MCI prevalence. Speech-based cognitive assessment has revolutionized early detection, with numerous studies showing that acoustic and linguistic changes in speech [3, 4]. Most speech-based diagnosis approaches are for developed countries, which is impossible for older Vietnamese, especially in rural and isolated locations, who have poor or low education due to previous educational restrictions and socioeconomic issues. Effective modifications for Mandarin Chinese, Japanese, and Spanish show that language-specific protocols can preserve diagnostic accuracy while resolving cultural and linguistic differences [5]. Vietnamese has six tones and unique phonetic and prosodic characteristics that differ from non-tonal languages, requiring language-specific modeling to capture tone variations and specific speech rhythm patterns that may be affected by cognitive decline [6]. Our Vietnamese speech-based screening methodology tackles these limitations by using cost-effective assessment, simple recording equipment available in community health clinics, and appropriate tasks for low-education individuals. This approach offers quantitative, objective measurement compared to subjective clinical observations, remote deployment and scalability across Vietnam's diverse healthcare infrastructure, and early detection before clinical dementia onset when interventions may be most effective.
