| Context
Dementia is a growing global health crisis, with 68% of the projected 140 million cases by 2050 expected in LMICs [1]. In rural Vietnam, individuals over 60 are disproportionately affected by Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) compared to their urban counterparts, highlighting a pressing need for targeted interventions [2]. MCI offers a critical two-to-three-year window where interventions can slow cognitive decline, making early treatment crucial [3]. Additionally, cardiovascular disease (CVD), a major risk factor for dementia [4], often goes undiagnosed in these rural areas [5]. Early interventions addressing both cognitive health and CVD have the potential to reduce the risk of developing dementia to prevent Alzheimer's progression, especially in regions with limited healthcare resources.
Objectives
The project's overall goal is to harness the collaboration's expertise to enable people at risk of dementia to benefit from technologies and monitoring that are culturally relevant and evidence-based. Specific objectives include:
Develop BrainTrain, a cognitive training app specifically tailored for MCI patients in rural Vietnam.
Design the app with a focus on accessibility and cultural appropriateness for older users with low technological proficiency and literacy.
Integrate telemonitoring features for cardiovascular health, including blood pressure (BP) and electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring, to address dementia risk factors.
Research Importances
The centralization of healthcare in urban Vietnam challenges rural populations' access to care. To bridge this gap, new infrastructures leveraging mobile technologies offer one solution. MCI and dementia are significant public health concerns in LMICs, and early interventions can delay Alzheimer’s onset, easing healthcare burdens and improving outcomes. With rising smartphone access [6], mobile-based interventions like BrainTrain offer scalable, cost-effective solutions suitable for these rural populations. Integrating CVDs monitoring, such as BP and ECG tracking, provides an opportunity to address cognitive and physical health. The project’s international collaboration between LMICs and high-income countries encourages knowledge sharing, enabling the development of adaptable interventions that are implementable across diverse settings.
Preliminary Research
We previously evaluated remote cognitive assessments of older Vietnamese adults using the MoCA-B and IQCODE. Development and analysis of the tool drew on the expertise of UK and US partners. Higher MCI and dementia rates were found in rural areas, with educational levels linked to cognitive function. These results indicate a need for developing non-verbal cognitive assessment methods for individuals with limited formal education.
We also designed cognitive games and tested them on 30 MCI patients. Participants were divided into high-and low-usage groups, with significant improvements observed in the high-usage group and an increase in MMSE scores (24.667 ± 1.989 to 27.400 ± 1.497). Patients' feedback reflected an average satisfaction score surpassing eight on a 10-point scale across all dimensions of the app. Since November 2023, we have recruited patients for the project and recently organized a community awareness program on Alzheimer’s at 175 Military Hospital. 157 MCI elderly patients have installed and used the BrainTrain app, with 91 of them still actively engaging with BrainTrain to date. These results demonstrate the potential of mobile-based cognitive training apps to enhance cognitive function in MCI patients. 
We demonstrated the feasibility of tele-BP monitoring devices in managing CVDs among MCI elderly in Vietnam. Over three months, 64.5% of participants remained engaged, and clinical outcomes improved, with fewer out-of-range BP readings compared to the control group. These findings suggest that telemonitoring may play a pivotal role in managing CVDs, a significant risk factor for dementia. This collaboration will extend this work to test the efficacy of technology-driven initiatives to support the care of older rural populations at risk of dementia and or cardiovascular disease.
Methodology
Design and Procedure
Seventy participants aged +60, half women half men from urban and rural areas, will be recruited for the study. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are listed in Table 1. 
Prior to the official intervention, in-depth interviews will ensure the BrainTrain app meets MCI patients’ needs. The intervention will consist of a three-month period with baseline, 1-month, and 3-month follow-ups (Fig. 1). During these check-ins, cognitive assessments, BP, ECG, and heart rate (HR) will be measured. Participants will be equipped with tele-BP monitoring and 3-lead ECG devices. Training will be provided for using the devices and the BrainTrain app at home. Collaborating clinicians will offer support during abnormal BP readings. 
Cognitive app acceptability and validations
Pilot study: Building on the previous version of BrainTrain (Preliminary Fig. P2, P3) and related work from the first network grant, the app will be optimized for rural, low-literacy populations. Improvements include refining game difficulty, adding daily engagement features, and enhancing the interface for ease of use by elderly participants with low literacy. In-depth interviews with MCI patients before home-based interventions will explore the usefulness and validity of the app as a resource for health. The app will be adapted in line with findings prior to implementation. 
Main study: In a three-month trial, participants will use BrainTrain for 25-30 minutes daily. Their data on daily usage, total training time, and cognitive performance will be tracked in a database. They will also complete a survey using a 5-point scale, adapted from the Mobile Application Rating Scale (MARS), to rate the app’s interface, user-friendliness, and engagement. Neuropsychological tests will assess cognitive changes before and after the intervention. 
Cardiovascular health indicators assessment
BP, HR and ECG recordings will be taken during cognitive tasks, followed by 24-hour Holter ECG monitoring to capture heart rate variability (HRV) parameters. HRV analysis will be performed on both the resting 12-lead ECG 5-min, tele-ECG session during cognitive tasks and 24h ECG recordings. The data will be exported into the HRV analysis software from ITR company for analysis of both time-domain and frequency-domain HRV parameters.
Milestones and Timescales:
Table 2 outlines the tentative schedule and milestones. The project will start with an international hybrid conference for feedback on the app and telemonitoring and involve international partners in the implementation strategy. A one-month pilot study follows, with regular online progress meetings, to prepare for the main study. It concludes with a final conference in Vietnam to present findings and agree on future directions. |

| The proposed activities aim to reduce the prevalence of AD by targeting the MCI stage in rural populations of Vietnam, a country on the DAC list. 
Employing a cognitive training app and telemonitoring of cardiovascular health, we address key risk factors associated with dementia. Early intervention mitigates the progression of AD, reducing the known risks of social exclusion. It facilitates the allocation of targeted health services and resources for marginalized groups, enabling older adults to continue contributing to family income and society. This supports economic sustainability by reducing the economic burden of caregiving duty, particularly for women who have to leave work to care for affected relatives.

| Gender equality in healthcare is a critical issue, especially in the context of Alzheimer's disease, where significant disparities exist. These disparities are partly due to biological factors, such as hormonal changes post-menopause and underlying medical conditions like cardiovascular-induced Alzheimer's, which may accelerate neurodegeneration. The issue is further exacerbated by disparities in diagnosis and care for rural women, underscoring our research's aim to reduce gender inequality in healthcare, particularly in Alzheimer's disease research.
The lifetime risk of developing Alzheimer's at age 45 is twice as high for women (20%) compared to men (10%) (according to the Alzheimer's Association). Women's longer life expectancy and postmenopausal hormonal changes increase their risk. Hormonal changes also elevate the risk of conditions like heart disease, which is linked to dementia. Therefore, our proposed intervention integrates cardiovascular health management to address these interconnected risks, ensuring more personalized and early treatment options.
The issue of gender inequality in health is particularly pronounced in rural areas, where women face additional challenges such as low literacy rates, limited access to information, and insufficient healthcare. These barriers exacerbate their risk of dementia, especially as lower education levels limit participation in mentally stimulating activities. Rural hospitals, often lacking specialized professionals, contribute to delayed diagnoses and treatment, further disadvantaging women. Our research on an integrated intervention aims to address these gaps and reduce the burden on caregivers and society. Preliminary data from the pilot show that women experience symptoms earlier and participate more actively, highlighting their concern for dementia. Expanding access to this intervention could help protect their health more effectively, regardless of location. |
