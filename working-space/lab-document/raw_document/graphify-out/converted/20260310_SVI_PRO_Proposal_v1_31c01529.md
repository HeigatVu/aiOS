SYDNEY VIETNAM INSTITUTE SEED GRANT
Project title:

PROJECT SUMMARY (250 words): viết cuối cùng
*** Viết tóm tắt dự án làm gì
Dementia affects over 57 million individuals worldwide, primarily in low- and middle-income countries like Vietnam, where cases double every 20 years. Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), often preceded by Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), accounts for most cases. Due to limited access to expensive diagnostic methods in rural Vietnam, speech analysis presents a promising, affordable, and non-invasive method for early detection of cognitive decline. However, Vietnam currently lacks a standardized speech protocol, dataset, and a set of validated features to support research and clinical screening in its native language. Therefore, this project aims to develop a primary method that utilizes speech to effectively support clinicians in neurodegenerative disease screening. The study will have two phases: phase 1 involves protocol design and piloting the study, while phase 2 will focus on recruiting 150 participants for cognitive assessment and speech task collection. Acoustic and linguistic features from speech tasks will be extracted to differentiate between MCI and AD.
This protocol and set of validated features will serve as a baseline for the future scale-up of this project in North and Central Vietnam. Additionally, the resulting database will provide a foundation for developing future machine learning-based tools aimed at the screening and monitoring of AD and MCI in Vietnam. Furthermore, the protocol and data will be shared with Southeast Asian countries in the region in the Asian Society Against Dementia (ASAD), which serves as a guide to build similar protocols, helping to make healthcare fairer for everyone.

How does the proposed research address a significant problem for Vietnam? (up to 200 words) (Nhân)
By 2049, Vietnam’s elderly population is projected to reach 19.6 million, accounting for 18.1% of the population [1], with an estimated 2.4 million individuals living with dementia by 2050 [2]. Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) is responsible for 60-70% of dementia cases [3], typically preceded by the Mild Cognitive Impairment stage (MCI) [4]. MCI affects more individuals over 60 in rural Vietnam than in urban areas, highlighting the critical need for early detection [5]. Currently, accurate diagnostic methods such as MRI and PET scans are costly and unevenly distributed, making them largely inaccessible in rural Vietnam [6, 7]. Therefore, speech analysis has emerged as a promising, non-invasive, and affordable tool for the early detection of cognitive decline [8]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that changes in speech, such as longer pauses between words and slower speech in memory tasks, and word properties can serve as early indicators of neurodegeneration [9-11]. However, Vietnam lacks a suitable speech procedure and dataset. This project develops the first Vietnamese speech protocol and set of acoustic and linguistic features for classifying MCI and AD to fill this gap. This research provides the foundation for early diagnosis in low-resource settings and public health screening.


What is (are) the research question(s) that this proposal will address? (up to 100 words)

This research proposal addresses the central question: To what extent can speech analysis accurately differentiate between Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) and Healthy Controls (HC) within the Vietnamese population?. To further clarify my investigation, the project specifically aims to answer the following research goals:
Examine the feasibility of developing a speech-based assessment on AD and MCI patients in Vietnam.
Identify the acoustic and linguistic features that differentiate among AD, MCI and HC within specific speech tasks.
Describe the research design and methods for the project that will be funded by this grant (up to 200 words)
Phase 1: Protocol development.
After reviewing the literature to identify reliable text and image stimuli for speaking tasks, we will build a draft version of speech protocol. Then our specialists give advice to  improve the exam (the interview guide of the appendix shows our first protocol). After the Military Hospital 175 approves research ethics. Subsequently, a pilot study with 5 participants in each group (AD, MCI, and HC) following the criteria (table 1 of the appendix) is conducted to identify protocol, method, and management concerns (figure 1 of the appendix). After fixing pilot study issues, we revise our protocol to improve our primary study.
Phase 2: Data collection and analysis.
The project will recruit 150 participants aged 55+ with criteria (shown in table 1 of the appendix) at Military Hospital 175 in Ho Chi Minh City. Then they will be allocated into AD, MCI and HC. We will request their consent to participate in this study and record their voice during the tasks. Then we will extract features from each speech task (shown in Table 2 of the appendix). Finally, we validate AD and MCI classification using acoustic and linguistic data. Figure 2 shows all information in phase 2.

How will the track record and expertise of the research team provide the relevant skills required to ensure the project is successful? (up to 100 words)
Our multidisciplinary team includes experts in AI, speech processing, neuroscience, neuropsychology, and clinical practice. We have 6-year of experience in translational research. For instance, we developed the “BrainTrain” application for cognitive decline with Military Hospital 175 and successfully validated the MoCA-B and IQCODE cognitive assessments for elderly Vietnamese. Furthermore, we built a machine learning model for detecting Alzheimer's Disease by speech analysis and have an extensive literature review about recent developments in acoustic and language-based analysis in AD. Combining the aforementioned experiments assists in the development of a reliable protocol for the early detection of cognitive decline.



Describe how this project will lead to an outstanding application for external research funding (up to 200 words)
This project will establish two significant and long-term contributions: (1) the first Vietnamese speech database for Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and healthy control and (2) a validated acoustic and linguistic feature for classifying AD and MCI. Besides, foundational contributions address an urgent public health need for accessible early detection of AD/ MCI through speech. Furthermore, we intend to use these foundational resources not only for this research but also as a pilot study for future, more substantial grant applications.
The dataset, protocol, and set of features will serve as a pilot for larger-scale research. Which may include:
Other research based on this protocol will be conducted for the central and north sides of Vietnam.
A longitudinal study tracks the progression of disease through speech.
A multi-modal project integrates speech data with other biomarkers, such as neuroimaging and blood tests.
An AI-based mobile screening tool will be developed for low-resource settings.
What outputs and/or outcomes do you expect to arise from this project? What impact do you expect the project will have in Southeast Asia? (up to 200 words)
This project will produce three primary outputs:
A protocol and speech data for research about Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) and mild cognitive impairment (MCI) in the Vietnamese elderly population.
A validated dataset of acoustic and linguistic speech data for classifying AD and MCI.
Young researchers with abilities in data collection, speech analysis, and interdisciplinary approaches to dementia research.
A manuscript to to be submitted to Q2 SCI-E journal
At the region's level, other countries can base their efficient speech tool or research on this field. Besides, this project will significantly contribute to the development of low-cost and accessible tools in Vietnam and Southeast Asian countries. As a member of BrainConnect, I will share my team's protocol, speech data, and the dataset of acoustic and linguistic features with regional partners via the Asian Society Against Dementia (ASAD). This work will provide a blueprint for developing similar tools for other languages in the region. Additionally, we can leverage this outcome to promote novel approaches for dementia patient care in the region.
